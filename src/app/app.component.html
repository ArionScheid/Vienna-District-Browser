Vienna District Browser

How it works Source on GitHub ↗

Which of Vienna's 23 districts fits the way you want to live? This app turns the public-transport timetable and a sample of rental listings into a knowledge graph, reasons over it, and ranks the districts by what matters to you: affordable rent, direct lines to the centre, or a dense station network.

How it works

An offline Python pipeline builds the graph and pre-computes everything; this page is a static site that only loads the small results. The numbers below come from the current build.

  1. 1 · Data

    Wiener Linien timetable (GTFS) with {{ m.graph.stations | number }} stations and {{ m.graph.lines }} lines, {{ m.graph.listings }} rental listings, official district borders.

  2. 2 · Knowledge graph

    An OWL ontology of districts, stations, lines, transport modes and listings, populated with {{ m.graph.asserted_triples | number }} RDF triples.

  3. 3 · Reasoning

    SPARQL rules infer which lines serve a district and which districts connect without changing (+{{ m.graph.inferred_triples | number }} triples). Queries then derive the district features.

  4. 4 · Embeddings

    Knowledge-graph embeddings (DistMult) of the line–district graph, clustered with k-means into {{ m.clustering.k }} "transit zones" (silhouette {{ m.clustering.silhouette | number:'1.2-2' }}).

  5. 5 · Graph neural network

    A GCN over the district graph learns to reproduce the match score for any preference (rank correlation ρ = {{ m.gnn.spearman_mean | number:'1.2-2' }} on unseen preferences).

The match score

Each district gets three components between 0 and 1: affordable rent (median rent per m², inverted), direct lines to the centre (daytime lines that also serve Innere Stadt), and a dense station network (stations per km²). Your sliders become weights that sum to 100 %, and the score is their weighted average. The map always uses this transparent formula; the GNN is the course's learning component and is evaluated against it in the notebooks.

Limitations

  • The rent sample is small (about a hundred of the cheapest listings on one day), so rent levels are a relative indication, not market prices. Districts with fewer than 3 listings count as "average".
  • Accessibility counts direct lines, not travel times or frequencies.
  • The scores compare Vienna's districts with each other; they are not absolute ratings.
